For parents

Wondering if your child's speech is on track?

Every child learns to talk at their own pace. If something doesn't feel right, it's worth asking.

At any age, it's worth asking if

  • Your child stops using words or skills they used to have
  • By age 2, your child uses fewer than 50 words or isn't putting two words together
  • By age 4, people outside the family understand less than half of what they say
  • They get frustrated trying to tell you something
  • You have a feeling something isn't clicking yet

What to look for

A parent's guide to speech and language from 18 months to 8 years and up, and when it's worth asking for help.

What to expect, by age

Children learn to talk at different speeds, so these are typical skills, not deadlines. If your child isn't doing something yet, it doesn't mean something is wrong. It means it's worth asking.

Choose an age on the trail to see what is typical. On a phone, you can also swipe the card.

Around 18 months

Words

Says about 50 words on average. Uses gestures to get a message across, like pointing to show you something, waving, and shaking their head "no."

Understanding

Follows simple one-step directions, like "give me the ball."

Play

Plays mostly on their own and watches other children play. Starts pretending with their own body, like pretending to sleep or drinking from an empty cup. Uses everyday things the right way, like a spoon or a hairbrush.

Around 2 years

Words

Says about 200 to 300 words and puts two words together, like "more milk."

Understanding

Follows two-step directions, like "get your shoes and bring them to me."

Speech sounds and clarity

Family understands much of what they say. Strangers often don't yet, and that's typical.

Play

Plays beside other children more than with them. Pretends with dolls and stuffed animals, like feeding a doll or tucking it in with a blanket, and acts out home life, like cooking dinner. Each pretend action is short and stands on its own.

Around 2½ years

Words

Says about 450 words. Uses words like "I," "me" and "we," and joins words with an action word, like "doggie run."

Speech sounds and clarity

Uses sounds like p, b, m, n, d, h and w in many words.

Play

Still plays mostly beside other children and starts copying what they do. Acts out things they've seen less often, like a visit to the doctor or a day at school. Needs toys that look like the real thing, and switches roles quickly.

Around 3 years

Words

Says about 1,000 words. Uses short sentences and asks "who," "what," "where" and "why" questions.

Understanding

Answers simple "what" and "where" questions, like "Where is your cup?" Follows directions with "in," "on" and "under."

Speech sounds and clarity

Says p, b, m, n, d, h and w clearly. Strangers understand a little under half of what they say, on average.

Play

Starts playing with other children: sharing toys and talking during play. Pretend play now has steps in order, like mixing a cake, baking it, serving it and washing the dishes. The story grows as they play instead of being planned.

Around 4 years

Words

Says about 1,500 words and sentences of four or more words. Tells you about something that happened in their day.

Understanding

Follows directions with location words like "behind," "next to" and "in front of."

Speech sounds and clarity

Adds t, k, g, f, y and the "ng" in "sing." Strangers understand at least half of what they say.

Play

Plays with other children toward a shared idea and takes turns. Uses one thing to stand for another, like a block as a phone, and gives dolls and puppets their own voices. Starts planning play ahead, and builds things they've seen out of blocks, like a house or a bridge.

Around 5 years

Words

Says about 2,000 words or more. Tells a story with a beginning and end, and keeps a conversation going.

Understanding

Follows directions with three steps. Understands order words like "first," "next" and "last," and time words like "yesterday" and "tomorrow."

Speech sounds and clarity

Adds s, z, l, v, sh, ch and j. Strangers understand at least 75% of what they say. Sounds like r and th often keep developing until 6 or 7.

Play

Plays together with other children toward a shared goal, including games with rules. Plans a pretend story ahead of time and gathers the toys and friends they need, like "You be the doctor." Can imagine a whole scene without real-looking toys.

Ages 6 to 8 and up

Words

Tells longer stories with details in order, explains how and why, and uses sentences with words like "because" and "if." Vocabulary keeps growing through reading and school.

Understanding

Follows longer directions in the classroom and starts to understand jokes, riddles and words with more than one meaning.

Speech sounds and clarity

Says all speech sounds clearly, including r and th, by about age 7. Strangers understand nearly everything they say.

Play

Plays team and board games with rules, works out the rules with friends, and handles winning and losing.

At any age, it's worth asking if

  • By age 2, your child uses fewer than 50 words or isn't putting two words together
  • By age 4, people outside the family understand less than half of what they say
  • They get frustrated trying to tell you something

Milestones adapted from: ASHA's Developmental Milestones, CDC's "Learn the Signs. Act Early." checklists, Crowe and McLeod (2020), Hustad and colleagues (2021), LinguiSystems Guide to Communication Milestones, Parten (1932), and Westby's Symbolic Play Scale (2000).

Why ask now

"Wait and see" is common advice. Here's what waiting can't tell you.

  • Many late talkers catch up. Some don't.

    Many children who talk late catch up by ages 3 to 5. Others go on to have trouble with language or reading, and early on it is hard to tell which child is which.

  • An evaluation gives you an answer.

    You leave with one of two things: peace of mind that your child is on track, or a clear plan for what to work on.

  • We work alongside school and state services.

    Therapy with Little Mountain Voices can work together with speech services your child gets at school or through other public programs. We coordinate with your child's team so everyone is working toward the same goals.

How your child says sounds and words

Services

Every child starts with an evaluation, so therapy is built around what your child needs.

Speech sounds

Ages 3 to 10

For children who are hard to understand, leave sounds out, or swap one sound for another. We work on clear speech through play and practice.

  • Weekly sessions at home or by teletherapy
  • Short practice to do between visits

Early language

Ages 18 months to 5, and older children when needed

For toddlers and preschoolers who are late to talk or use fewer words than expected. Parents learn simple ways to build language into the day.

  • Sessions at home, with a parent or caregiver taking part
  • Ideas that fit into meals, play and bedtime

Evaluations

New clients and second opinions

A full look at your child's speech and language, followed by a written report and a clear plan you can share with your child's doctor or school.

Experience with autism

How it works

Flexible scheduling, one therapist, and an optional monthly plan.

Why Little Mountain Voices

Therapy that fits around your family

Many practices lock you into one day and time. We don't.

  • Scheduling that flexes

    Your session time can change from week to week. Pick what works this week, and something different next week.

  • An optional monthly plan

    If you'd like, one steady monthly payment can cover your child's sessions, instead of a bill after every visit.

  • We come to you

    At home, out in the community, or by teletherapy. Wherever your child does best.

  • One therapist, every visit

    Your child works with Kodee each time. No rotating staff.

  • Parents are part of it

    You see what we work on and leave each session knowing how to help.

From first call to weekly sessions

  1. Free phone call

    A 15-minute call to hear your concerns and see whether we're the right fit.

  2. Evaluation

    Kodee meets your child at home, then sends a written report and plan.

  3. Weekly sessions

    The same therapist every week, for 45 to 60 minutes. The day and time don't have to be the same: each week you can choose from the open times, so sessions fit around your family's schedule.

  4. Practice at home

    Each visit ends with a few things to try before the next one.
